# Adaptive trimmed-plane research, not enabled in the app

An independent in-memory OCCT experiment on both annular planar faces of the first solid of the owner's Lego STEP found usable G1 poles with a 35 × 35 net. It derived a narrow band from the inner contour's UV bounds, added 48 knots, and bounded circular outer edges using π/48 subdivisions. One 0.02 mm pole move on each face yielded valid STEP and matching recipe-replay volumes; the other nine faces and both boundary wires were sampled, with maximum boundary differences below 8e-13 mm. A box face without a hole was refused. This is only two faces of one part and one moved pole per face, not UI or general topology qualification.

`adaptiveTrimPlan.ts` is a standalone bounded candidate generator with unit tests. It does **not** prepare, select or deform any CAD face and is not connected to the worker or UI. Its factors are heuristics. A caller must independently validate topology, visible influence, protected boundaries, CAD validity, resource costs and preservation of all other geometry. It must fail without replacing the active draft.

An independent review identified a compatibility requirement: the current v4/v5/v6 recipes do not encode the automatic trim-lock policy. Do not silently change their π/12 protection when replaying them; the finer policy needs an explicitly versioned recipe or equivalent persisted contract, plus legacy replay regression tests and coordinator/host integration. Sampling does not prove continuous G1. Performance on a dense net, a broad variety of trimmed STEP faces and packaged desktop remain unqualified.

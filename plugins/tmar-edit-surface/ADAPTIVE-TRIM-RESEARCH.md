# Adaptive trimmed-plane research, not enabled in the app

An independent in-memory OCCT experiment on both annular planar faces of the first solid of the owner's Lego STEP found unlocked G1 poles with a 35 × 35 net. It derived a narrow band from the inner contour's UV bounds, added 48 knots, and bounded circular outer edges using π/48 subdivisions. One 0.02 mm pole move on each face yielded valid STEP and matching recipe-replay volumes; the other nine faces and both boundary wires were sampled, with maximum boundary differences below 8e-13 mm. A box face without a hole was refused. **Visible influence was not measured**, so these moves do not yet establish useful editing. This is only two faces of one part and one moved pole per face, not UI or general topology qualification.

On the first annular face (index 3), a separate diagnostic of the existing **uniform** fallback `c532d18` found 12 G1-unlocked poles, but no sampled interior witness in their support. The classifier found 20,962 interior samples on a 256 × 256 grid; after moving the first unlocked pole (72) by 0.02 mm, the maximum displacement at those interior samples was 7.26e-13 mm. The output remained a valid solid. This measures one pole and sampled points only, but shows that a valid output and unlocked pole do not establish a visible deformation. Keep functional qualification on hold. Reproduce with `SURFACE_QA_STEP` and `tests/realDenseVisibleWitness.test.ts`; the adaptive candidate itself has not yet been assessed by this test.

`adaptiveTrimPlan.ts` is a standalone bounded candidate generator with unit tests. It does **not** prepare, select or deform any CAD face and is not connected to the worker or UI. Its factors are heuristics. A caller must independently validate topology, visible influence, protected boundaries, CAD validity, resource costs and preservation of all other geometry. It must fail without replacing the active draft.

The opt-in `tests/realAdaptiveVisibleWitness.test.ts` exercises that generator on the two Lego faces using an **in-memory-only** Vite transform from π/12 to π/48 to create the experimental draft. With `SURFACE_QA_STEP`, `SURFACE_QA_ADAPTIVE_PROBE=1` and `SURFACE_QA_TRANSIENT_ARC48=1`, it measured interior displacement of 0.00999767 mm (face 3) and 0.00999478 mm (face 7) after a 0.02 mm pole move. On each face, 100 boundary samples differed by at most 6.05e-13 mm, sampled boundary normals matched, and 72 samples on other faces did not move; both exported STEP solids were valid and the source hash unchanged. The serialized v7 recipe was replayed both through a test-only adapter in the transient kernel and through the real kernel's isolated `replayAdaptiveRecipe` API; the sampled edited points matched. The isolated API rejected wrong body IDs, invalid policy and altered base-net hashes, while the legacy reader rejected v7. **The v7 API is not exposed to the worker or application**. This particular test creates its recipe with the transient adapter; direct kernel creation is assessed separately below. Continuous boundaries, application save/reopen, runtime performance and UI remain unqualified. Without the transient transform the current π/12 experiment fails on face 7; neither path is enabled as a product feature.

The native replay output was also checked for validity, volume, other-face samples, boundary points and boundary normals. The isolated fine policy rejects non-planar supports, more than one inner wire and non-circular outer edges; it does not prove that several circular arcs are concentric or that an arbitrary trim is suitable. An independent review identified a compatibility requirement: the current v4/v5/v6 recipes do not encode the automatic trim-lock policy. Their π/12 protection remains unchanged; the finer policy uses a separate v7 contract. Sampling does not prove continuous G1. Performance on a dense net, a broad variety of trimmed STEP faces and packaged desktop remain unqualified.

The kernel now also has isolated experimental `selectAdaptiveExperimental` and `createAdaptiveRecipe` entry points. On both Lego faces, `tests/realAdaptiveKernel.test.ts` creates and replays a v7 edit without any transient module transform and verifies a measurable internal displacement, valid STEP, replay point agreement and unchanged source. The candidate requires at least one G1-unlocked pole with a sampled rational basis contribution above 1%; this is a witness, not a guarantee that every displayed pole usefully affects the trimmed face. Ordinary `createRecipe` rejects an adaptive selection rather than saving it with the legacy policy. A newer preview invalidates a recipe still awaiting hashes, including legacy recipe creation. V7 locked poles are compared to the original net: locking a pole after moving it is currently refused and requires a lock-at-current-value contract before UI exposure. These entry points remain absent from worker/UI and have not passed application integration or broad geometry qualification.

An independent **v8** experimental contract stores the position of each pole at the time it was locked, leaving v7 unchanged. `adaptiveLockSnapshot.ts` captures and validates immutable lock positions; kernel creation rejects a moved-after-lock net and replay rejects mismatched saved coordinates. On both real Lego faces the isolated test moves a pole, locks it, creates v8, replays it and checks the reconstructed surface point, STEP validity and volume, other-face samples, and sampled boundary positions and normals; inconsistent snapshots are rejected. These consistency checks do not authenticate a document if geometry and references are changed together. The draft and history have an explicit experimental opt-in: unit tests exercise lock, undo, redo, unlock and relock; preparation changes are rejected until locks can be remapped, before sending refinement to the worker. The worker has separate experimental select/evaluate/create/replay/restore actions fenced by a monotonically increasing selection generation. A real STEP worker-dispatch test checks stale requests, retained state after rejected restore, successful restore of locked values, and creation of another recipe from the restored selection. Concurrent session change during a kernel restore has not been directly tested. This is not a browser or packaged-app test: the draft flag does not itself certify the worker policy, draft restoration and the UI do not yet expose these actions. Real application undo/redo and save/reopen remain unqualified.

The experimental draft now requires the matching worker selection identity and routes preview and STEP evaluation to the adaptive worker action. The preview identity includes that selection; free-boundary and unsupported tangency and legacy save paths are refused. The real STEP worker-dispatch test exercises draft installation, preview and STEP evaluation; 23 focused draft/runtime tests and TypeScript also passed. This remains experimental: application integration and UI are not connected. Do not expose it as a completed feature.

An experimental draft v8 save now checks the returned face, net, hash and lock snapshots against the current draft, and refreshes its CAD preview after the worker operation. The LEGO worker-dispatch test exercises save with a locked point; a focused test checks that editing is blocked while saving and that a worker refusal leads to a new preview. This does not connect project persistence or draft restore, establish authenticity, or qualify the UI. Dispose during hashing and a forged inconsistent worker reply have not been directly tested.

The worker's adaptive selection identity now includes the trim-protected and hidden pole lists. The draft refuses lists that disagree with that identity; the worker rejects an altered identity. A real STEP worker-dispatch test checks the rejection and the restored selection, and the draft tests exercise a mismatch. The worker clears old selection identities if its committed restore response unexpectedly lacks a preparation. Host integration, application-level restore, and proof against coordinated tampering remain outside this experiment.

The draft has an experimental v8 restore method. It verifies the returned geometry, lock positions and protected-pole identity, confirms the new worker selection by evaluating it, and reconstructs history only after those checks. The real STEP worker-dispatch test saves, restores, saves again, rejects an invalid STEP with the draft blocked as unverified, and restores the valid source again. Nine focused draft tests and TypeScript pass. This conservative failure path may require reselection even when a failed worker restore retained its previous state. Failure after worker commit, disposal during awaits and application project save/reopen remain unqualified; this does not expose the feature in the UI.

The workspace session now has an explicit experimental adaptive selection option, prepares v8 history, accepts v8 restore, and the independent history replay recognizes v8. A lost selection response closes the affected session conservatively; changing selection policy on the same face is no longer silently ignored. The 64 session/replay tests include fake-worker coverage of the adaptive dispatch and failure, plus legacy real-kernel coverage; they do not establish an end-to-end adaptive session or host persistence. The UI does not expose the option, and the host must qualify its v8 transaction and annotated-note handling before enabling it.

An opt-in LEGO STEP test now exercises an adaptive `WorkspaceSurfaceSession` with the real dispatcher and kernel: select, move an unlocked witness pole, await the new valid preview, lock it, prepare v8, dispose the controller and restore into a new controller. The restored poles and lock match. The host and transport are simulated, and the second controller reuses the same in-memory worker implementation and kernel; no browser, independent process restart, project file or pointer drag is tested. The source STEP is read only. This is not application qualification.

The same session test now places a note on the modified face, saves a v8 annotated history bundle, and restores the note along with the geometry and lock. The restored text matches and its 3D position differs by less than 1e-6 mm in this case. The same simulated host/transport and reused kernel limitations apply: this is not a persisted project, actual browser interaction or general note qualification.

The experimental session reports refinement, G1 support selection and free boundaries unavailable while the adaptive policy is active, matching the existing draft restrictions instead of presenting commands that will fail. A session test checks the reported availability; this does not implement those operations for adaptive faces.

Actual worker restart, browser and project qualification remain unverified; the failed launch was not retried.

An experimental kernel-only adaptive refinement now inserts knots in an already deformed net while comparing the refined base and trim protection, and evaluates the candidate in an isolated CAD editor before changing the active selection. Manual locks are refused; the caller must enforce the lock state itself. On LEGO face 7, one insertion after a 0.02 mm pole move preserved a sampled interior witness within 1e-6 mm and produced a valid solid with matching volume; a manual-lock refusal retained a valid previous preview. This is one sample on one face, not a continuity or general boundary proof. STEP round-trip of the isolated source may conservatively reject valid cases. Worker, undo/redo, persistence, UI and manual-lock remapping are not connected or qualified.

# Curved-support G1 research checkpoint

An isolated mathematical helper proposes and validates G1 along a straight natural common edge of compatible, nonrational clamped B-spline patches. It changes the selected patch's first interior row only and rejects locks, incompatible bases and cases where regularity cannot be certified conservatively. It is not connected to the CAD editor, worker, recipe, or user interface; CAD topology and preservation must be checked by a future caller.

Eight mathematical tests and three real-OCCT fixture tests passed. The fixture's support normal varies along the shared edge; after correction, the sewn STEP shell remains valid and 33 independent samples show seam position deviation below 1e-8 mm and sine of normal angle below 1e-9. These sample results concern only the synthetic fixture. The helper's conservative coefficient bounds cover its compatible mathematical class, not arbitrary NURBS, trimmed surfaces or global model G1.

The next integration requires independent topological identification of the support and natural edge, CAD readback and preservation of nonselected faces, session identity, Undo/Redo, recipe replay and UI verification. No QuickSurface parity or production feature is claimed.

Follow-up: a standalone CAD topology recognizer now verifies source identity, unambiguous adjacency, linear natural UV boundaries and native nonrational B-spline support nets. Seven tests passed on synthetic OCCT geometry and numeric boundary cases. A review found the original parameter allowance could accept a one-unit discrepancy in a large-origin numerical probe; a separate CAD example was already rejected by another guard, so a prior end-to-end false positive was not established. The corrected allowance is bounded by knot spans and a conservative 1e-8 mm geometric estimate. Further independent review found no additional concrete defect. The recognizer remains unconnected to editor/kernel actions and does not support arbitrary trims, rational surfaces or periodic seams.

Further follow-up: the kernel can list and resolve compatible curved supports and propose/validate a modification in isolation. The test uses an imported synthetic STEP shell with three faces, protects the existing preview and the other faces, verifies the result through STEP roundtrip, and exercises failure and revision fences. It covers a narrow nonrational natural-boundary class only. Twelve focused tests and fifteen planar regressions passed; non-finite readback and partially trimmed degree-one pcurves were explicitly rejected after independent review. Publication, live draft history, recipe persistence, UI and host integration of curved G1 remain unimplemented.

Kernel follow-up: publishing and re-evaluating an accepted curved G1 constraint now preserves the protected rows and initial locks while permitting deeper interior edits. Three tests using real OCCT on a synthetic 5×5 three-face STEP fixture passed, including witness/UV anchors, unchanged supporting and third faces, stale tokens and injected failures; four planar G1 regression tests also passed. Independent static review reported no concrete defect. This remains kernel-only: worker, draft/history, persistent recipe, UI, host integration, real scan and QuickSurface parity are unverified or absent.

Worker follow-up: the kernel actions are dispatched through the existing serialized worker protocol, with exact source selection and no automatic reselection. Real OCCT and actual dispatcher were tested through a local structured-clone transport: curved and planar tokens remain isolated, stale queued work is rejected, and a disposed client ignores late replies. Browser-worker termination was not qualified. Draft/history, curved recipes, UI and host integration remain absent.

Draft follow-up: the curved constraint now has its own preview queue and history state; internal edits, Undo/Redo, anchor witness and STEP export were tested through draft, worker dispatcher and real OCCT on a synthetic fixture. A planar integration regression also passed. Curved recipe creation explicitly refuses rather than losing the constraint. The workspace UI, annotated copy workflow and host integration remain unqualified; no QuickSurface parity is implied.
